# Unit 3: How Plants Make Food

## Lesson 1: Photosynthesis

### Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson the student can:
- Explain in their own words what photosynthesis is and why plants need it.
- Name the three things a plant takes in for photosynthesis and the two things it produces.
- Predict what happens to a plant that is kept in the dark.

### What is photosynthesis?

Plants cannot eat the way animals do. Instead, they make their own food. The process a plant uses to make food is called photosynthesis. The word comes from two Greek words: "photo," meaning light, and "synthesis," meaning putting together. Photosynthesis is putting together food using light.

Photosynthesis happens inside tiny structures in the leaf called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is what makes leaves green, and it is what captures the energy in sunlight.

### What goes in and what comes out

A plant needs three things for photosynthesis:
1. Sunlight, captured by chlorophyll.
2. Water, which the roots absorb from the soil and the stem carries up to the leaves.
3. Carbon dioxide, a gas the leaf takes in from the air through tiny openings called stomata.

Using the energy from sunlight, the plant combines water and carbon dioxide to make two things:
1. Glucose, a sugar. This is the plant's food. The plant uses some right away for energy and stores the rest as starch.
2. Oxygen, a gas. The plant releases oxygen into the air through the stomata. This is the oxygen that animals and people breathe.

In words: carbon dioxide + water, with light energy, makes glucose + oxygen.

### A common mistake

Many students think plants get their food from the soil. They do not. Plants get water and minerals from the soil, but the food itself, the sugar, is made in the leaves from carbon dioxide and water. A plant grown in only water with the right minerals and plenty of light will still grow, because it is still making its own food.

### Why it matters

Almost every living thing depends on photosynthesis. Animals eat plants, or eat animals that ate plants, so the energy in nearly all food started as sunlight captured by a leaf. And the oxygen we breathe was released by plants and algae doing photosynthesis.

### Think about it

- What would happen to a plant left in a dark closet for two weeks, even if it were watered? Why?
- Why are leaves flat and wide instead of round like a ball?
- If you covered one leaf of a plant with black paper for a week, what do you predict you would see when you removed the paper?

### Practice

1. List the three inputs and two outputs of photosynthesis.
2. Explain why a plant kept in the dark will eventually die, even if it has water.
3. A friend says, "Plants eat dirt." Explain what is wrong with this, using the word glucose.

## Lesson 2: Respiration in Plants

### Learning objectives

- Explain that plants, like animals, release energy from food by respiration.
- Compare respiration and photosynthesis.

### Plants need energy too

Making glucose is only half the story. To grow, move water, and build new leaves, a plant needs energy. Like animals, plants release the energy stored in glucose through a process called respiration. Respiration uses oxygen and glucose and produces carbon dioxide and water, along with energy the plant can use.

Notice that respiration is nearly the reverse of photosynthesis. Photosynthesis stores energy from light in glucose. Respiration releases that energy. Photosynthesis only happens in the light, because it needs light energy. Respiration happens all the time, day and night, because the plant always needs energy.

### Think about it

- During the day, a plant does both photosynthesis and respiration. At night, it does only one. Which one, and why?
- Does a plant give off more oxygen during the day or at night?

### Practice

1. Fill in the table comparing photosynthesis and respiration: what goes in, what comes out, when it happens.
2. Explain why a plant still needs oxygen even though it makes oxygen.
