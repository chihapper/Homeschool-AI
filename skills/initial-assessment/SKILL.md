# Initial assessment (intake)

Purpose: find out how to teach *this* learner well. Not to make things easier; to make learning stick.

## The conversation (aim for 8-12 exchanges)

1. **Warm up** (1-2 turns): name, age or grade, what they like doing. Keep it light.
2. **Prior knowledge probe** (2-3 turns): ask what they already know about the subject in their own words.
   Ask one follow-up "how do you know?" question.
3. **Intake preference probe** (2-3 turns): teach one tiny idea from the course two different ways
   (for example, a definition first, then an analogy) and ask which one clicked and why.
4. **Demonstration probe** (2-3 turns): ask them to show the idea back in two formats
   (explain in own words; then apply to a new case). Notice which produced clearer evidence.
5. **Challenge tolerance probe** (1-2 turns): ask a question they probably cannot answer yet, and
   observe: do they guess, freeze, ask for help, or reason aloud? Reassure afterwards.
6. **Close**: thank them, tell them one specific thing you noticed they did well.

## Producing the profile

- Every field must be justified by something the learner actually said or did. Quote it in `priorKnowledge`, `observedGaps`.
- `tutoringGuidance` is a list of at most six concrete instructions to the tutor, e.g. "Open with an example before naming the term", "Give 5+ seconds of silence before scaffolding", "Analogies to soccer land well". The tutor follows each one literally, so six it can follow beat eleven it cannot.
- `depth` is how much of an answer they are ready for, from what they showed, not their grade: `simple`
  (the plain version, one idea at a time) unless the challenge probe showed them reasoning aloud with a
  "why" or an "it depends" (`some-nuance`); `full` needs evidence an intake rarely gives. The profile
  pass revises it from later sessions.
- `parentSummary` is 3-5 plain sentences a parent can act on.
- Do not label the learner ("visual learner"). Describe observed preferences and evidence.
- Do not assign a gender. Use the learner's name, or a pronoun only if they or the parent used one in
  the conversation; otherwise they. A profile that says "his" without cause follows the child into
  every report.
