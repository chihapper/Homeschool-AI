# Research brief: Socratic AI tutoring for homeschool families

*Compiled 14 September 2026 from two research passes (product landscape and learning-science evidence; voice-latency and media-ingestion technology). Sources are linked inline. Items the researchers could not confirm against a primary source are marked **(unverified)**; several primary sites (dev.hume.ai, anthropic.com, nature.com, openai.com, artificialanalysis.ai) were blocked by this environment's network policy, so some figures come from secondary coverage and are marked **(snippet)**.*

## Executive summary

1. **Nobody has built this exact product.** Guided modes from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are prompt layers on general chatbots: no persistent learner model, no grounding in a family's own material, no parent-facing mastery report. Synthesis Tutor is the closest (voice-first Socratic, marketed to homeschoolers) but is K-5 math only. Khanmigo is Socratic but text-only and Khan-content-only. The all-in-one homeschool platforms (Time4Learning, Miacademy, Monarch, Power Homeschool) have no AI dialogue at all. Alpha School shows the failure mode to avoid: AI-generated curriculum that turned out to be poorly constructed.
2. **The evidence supports the thesis, with one correction.** Unrestricted AI answers make students worse on later unassisted tests (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025: 17% worse). Guardrailed, answer-withholding tutors avoid that harm (same study) and, when grounded in instructor-vetted content and kept brief, can beat active-learning classrooms (Kestin et al. 2025, effect sizes 0.73 to 1.3) and lift real classroom outcomes (Google LearnLM Sierra Leone RCT, +0.26 SD). The correction: **pure questioning is "minimal guidance," and minimal guidance fails complete novices.** The strongest design is *teach briefly, then question*, with the amount of guidance fading as the learner demonstrates knowledge. Productive-failure effects also reverse for grades 2 to 5, so young children should get instruction first.
3. **Voice latency is a real constraint and Opus 5 is the wrong default for the live tutor.** Human turn gaps are 0 to 200 ms modal, roughly 200 to 500 ms feels natural, and voice products feel laggy past about 1.5 s. Published time-to-first-token for Claude Opus 5 at low effort is around 2.7 s; Sonnet 5 around 0.8 to 1.4 s; Haiku 4.5 around 0.6 to 0.7 s (snippet figures). Hume EVI in custom-language-model mode adds one network hop on top. The build now defaults the tutor to Sonnet 5 in `.env.example`, keeps Opus 5 for the offline agents, and measures TTFT on every turn.
4. **Hume EVI with a custom language model is ASR + Claude + Hume's speech-language model "acting out" the text**, not native speech-to-speech. Native speech-to-speech (EVI 3's single model) would mean giving up the orchestrator, grounding, and assessor loop. Keeping CLM mode is the right call; the cost is latency, which the model choice above addresses.
5. **Ingestion is solved technically; YouTube is a terms-of-service question.** Server-side yt-dlp against YouTube violates YouTube's terms (civil, not criminal). TED talks are CC BY-NC-ND and the transcript is on the page. Podcast enclosures are publicly distributed and Podcasting 2.0 feeds often ship transcripts. PowerPoint is a zip of XML. The build handles all of them; the README now says to use yt-dlp only for content the family owns or is licensed to use, or to plug in a hosted transcript API.

## What changed in the build because of this research

- `skills/socratic-method`: a "guidance fades with knowledge" section (novice: teach briefly then ask; some knowledge: question first with a hint ladder; demonstrated knowledge: problem first), an answer-seeking rule, and a consolidation rule for wrap-up.
- `skills/learner-adaptation`: per-concept path selection, an age rule for grade 5 and below, and "speed is not success."
- `skills/mastery-assessment` and the report schema: `answerSeekingTurns` and `tutorAnswerGiveaways` counts, and hinted-versus-independent evidence.
- Orchestrator: spaced retrieval. A new session opens with one retrieval question on the least-recently-mastered concept from earlier reports.
- Latency: model-aware effort and thinking parameters, a second prompt-cache breakpoint, cache pre-warm on session start, per-turn TTFT measurement, and Sonnet 5 as the recommended voice tier. See `docs/LATENCY.md`.
- Ingestion: podcast transcript tags used before paying for speech-to-text; TED transcript read from the page before falling back to yt-dlp.

# Part A. Product landscape, evidence, and learning science

## 1. Product landscape

### 1a. General-purpose "guided" AI tutors

**Khanmigo (Khan Academy).** Text chat tutor tied to Khan Academy content; the core design principle is Socratic questioning rather than answer-giving. Pricing for families is $4/month or $44/year, free for teachers ([kidsaitools](https://www.kidsaitools.com/en/articles/khanmigo-review-2026), [toolcurrent](https://toolcurrent.com/tools/khanmigo)). It has speech-to-text and text-to-speech but not real-time conversational voice; one 2026 review calls it "text-only without real-time voice" ([kidsaitools](https://www.kidsaitools.com/en/articles/khanmigo-review-2026)). It accepts image uploads of problems but does not ingest a family's own curriculum; reviewers note "limited value outside the Khan ecosystem" and that writing/humanities tutoring is "significantly weaker" ([aiteacherhacks](https://aiteacherhacks.com/khanmigo-review/), [myengineeringbuddy](https://www.myengineeringbuddy.com/blog/khanmigo-reviews-alternatives-pricing-offerings/)). Khan Academy explicitly markets to homeschoolers via its help center ([support.khanacademy.org](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/36065978206221-How-can-I-use-Khan-Academy-to-homeschool-my-child)).

**OpenAI ChatGPT Study Mode** (launched July 29, 2025). Custom system instructions written with educators produce "guiding questions that calibrate responses to their objective and skill level," scaffolding, and checks for understanding ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/)). It inherits ChatGPT's file upload and voice mode (whether Study Mode behaviour persists in voice is unverified). OpenAI has since placed Study Mode inside teen parental controls ([winssolutions](https://www.winssolutions.org/chatgpt-study-mode-teen-controls/)) and published early outcomes research: microeconomics students using Study Mode scored roughly 15% higher on exams than peers relying on traditional online resources, with a "Learning Outcomes Measurement Suite" built with University of Tartu and Stanford SCALE ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/understanding-ai-and-learning-outcomes/), [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/openai-tracks-ai-student-performance-over-time)). The 15% figure is OpenAI's own and not peer-reviewed. Known weakness: it is a mode the student can switch off; it is a prompt layer, not a curriculum-grounded system ([Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/article/putting-chatgpts-study-mode-through-its-paces/)).

**Anthropic Claude Learning Mode.** Piloted in Claude for Education (April 2025), then rolled out to all users as a "Learning" style (August 2025); it "stops blurting out answers and starts asking clarifying questions" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/anthropic-brings-claudes-learning-mode-to-regular-users-and-devs-170018471.html), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/04/claude-for-education)). Anthropic's education lead framed it as combating "brain rot" from copy-paste use ([opentools](https://opentools.ai/news/anthropics-claude-ai-breaks-new-ground-with-socratic-learning-mode)). Works with Projects/file uploads and Claude's voice mode. Not child-targeted; no mastery tracking or parent view.

**Google Gemini Guided Learning / LearnLM.** Launched August 2025 as Google's answer to Study Mode ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)); LearnLM capabilities are folded into Gemini 2.5+ ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/google-gemini-learnlm-update/)). Uses "probing and open-ended questions" ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/)). Google is the only vendor with a large pre-registered RCT (see Section 2). Gemini Live provides voice. Free for students in many markets ([Gemini for Students](https://gemini.google/students/)). Not curriculum-grounded to a family's material; no learner profile beyond chat memory.

**Synthesis Tutor.** Voice-guided Socratic K-5 math tutor (ages 5-11), tablet-first, secular; explicitly marketed to homeschoolers and supplemental rather than a full curriculum ([homeschoolfox](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/synthesis-tutor), [homeschooltools.net](https://homeschooltools.net/offering/synthesis-tutor)). Reported pricing conflicts across sources ($99-$119/yr family plan up to 7 kids; other sources cite $35/mo), treat as unverified ([brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/synthesis-tutor-cost/)). Strong engagement reviews (87% five-star on Trustpilot per [aitoolsforkids](https://www.aitoolsforkids.com/blog/synthesis-tutor-review-ai-math-tutor-for-kids)). Math only; own-material ingestion: no. This is the closest existing product to a "voice-first Socratic tutor for homeschoolers," but only for K-5 math.

**Sizzle AI.** Free, voice-first STEM homework helper aimed at 10+, "asking follow-up questions and adjusting explanations" ([classwork.com](https://classwork.com/ai-tutoring-is-expensive-sizzle-is-free/), [aichief](https://aichief.com/ai-education-tools/sizzle-ai/)). Reviewers criticise vague marketing and lack of demos; it is fundamentally a step-by-step solver with conversational wrapping, not mastery-tracked.

**Ello.** AI reading tutor that listens to a child read aloud and corrects decoding; $14.99/mo or $139/yr, iOS only ([spellingjoy](https://spellingjoy.com/best-apps/app/ello)). Complaints: glitches, sentence-jumping, weak parental controls ([learningreadinghub](https://learningreadinghub.com/blog/phonics/read-with-ello-reading-app-review/)). Voice is used for listening, not dialogue. **Amira Learning** is the district-sold equivalent with the strongest evidence (claimed effect size ~0.70 on reading growth, vendor-funded and contested) but no self-serve family pricing ([Amira research](https://amiralearning.com/research), [critique](https://sfeducation.substack.com/p/no-amiras-ai-tutor-is-not-ready-to)).

**Speak.** Voice-first AI language tutor, ~$18-20/mo, Premium Plus ~$180/yr ([speakshark](https://speakshark.com/blog/speak-app-pricing-per-month-2026)). Relevant only as a reference implementation of real-time spoken tutoring with error targeting.

**Photomath / Socratic by Google.** Direct-answer OCR solvers. Socratic was removed from app stores in October 2024 and folded into Google Lens ([nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/socratic-app)). These are the "GPT Base" archetype: they are what the Bastani evidence (Section 2) warns about.

### 1b. Homeschool-specific platforms

**Time4Learning, Miacademy, Power Homeschool (Acellus), Monarch (AOP).** All-in-one self-paced video/interactive curricula with auto-grading. 2026 pricing: Time4Learning $24.95-$39.95/mo per child (sources disagree) ([brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/time4learning-cost/), [Time4Learning](https://www.time4learning.com/pages/membership-pricing-discounts)); Miacademy from $43/mo ([brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/miacademy-cost/)); Monarch ~$40/mo or $400-450/yr ([brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/monarch-homeschool-reviews/)); Power Homeschool $99/mo ([brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/power-homeschool-cost/)). None offers a conversational AI tutor as of these reviews; pedagogy is lecture + quiz. They own the curriculum; families cannot bring their own.

**Alpha School / 2 Hour Learning.** The most-hyped AI-first model; claims "2X in 2 hours" via app-based mastery learning with "guides" instead of teachers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_School)). Independent reporting is damaging: a February 2026 404 Media investigation based on leaked documents found AI-generated lessons "poorly constructed," with "illogical multiple-choice questions," internally flagged as sometimes doing "more harm than good," remote coaches in the Philippines and Colombia, and webcam monitoring on by default ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/students-are-being-treated-like-guinea-pigs-inside-an-ai-powered-private-school/), [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2026/03/23/alpha-school-ai)). IXL stated Alpha's use of its product violated its terms of service ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_School)). Researchers note the 2.6x claim cannot be evaluated because data and selection are not public ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320040/20260709/ai-private-schools-promise-twice-learning-experts-cannot-verify-that-claim.htm), [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/ai-schools-like-alpha-promise-efficiency-but-cant-replicate-the-messy-process-that-helps-kids-learn-282464)). Lesson for this product: AI-generated curriculum is the weak link; grounding in parent-chosen material sidesteps it.

## 2. Evidence on AI tutoring

**Bloom's 2 sigma and its revision.** Bloom's 1984 claim that one-to-one tutoring plus mastery learning yields a 2 SD gain rested on two small dissertations and was never replicated; it conflated tutoring with mastery learning ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom's_2_sigma_problem)). Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan's meta-analysis of RCTs found a pooled human tutoring effect of ~0.37 SD ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27476/w27476.pdf)); the 2024 published version reports 0.288 SD, and none of 96 studies reached 2 sigma ([Nickow et al. 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00028312231208687)). Mastery learning itself averages d ≈ 0.52 across 108 evaluations, strongest for weaker students ([Kulik et al. 1990](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265)). Realistic bar for any tutor: 0.2-0.5 SD.

**Kestin et al. 2025 (Harvard, Scientific Reports).** N=194, crossover RCT in an intro physics course, Fall 2023; AI tutor at home vs. active-learning classroom on identical content. AI group learned more than twice as much; effect sizes 0.73-1.3 (quantile regression); median session 49 min vs. 60 min class; engagement 4.1 vs 3.6/5 ([Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6), [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/), [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/)). Design features: brief responses to manage cognitive load, "DO NOT give away the full solution," growth-mindset tone, timely targeted feedback, self-pacing, and pre-vetted instructor content ([ETC Journal](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)). The authors noted the prompt alone "could not reliably provide enough structure to scaffold problems with multiple parts." Caveats: single institution, single short intervention, immediate post-test only.

**Bastani et al. 2025 (PNAS, Turkey).** ~1,000 high-school math students, RCT of GPT-4 "Base" (plain ChatGPT) vs. "Tutor" (teacher-designed hints, no answers). Practice performance rose 48% (Base) and 127% (Tutor), but on the unassisted exam Base students did **17% worse** than no-AI controls while Tutor students were statistically indistinguishable from controls ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/), [psypost](https://www.psypost.org/unrestricted-generative-ai-harms-high-school-math-learning-by-acting-as-a-crutch/)). Students in the Base condition largely asked for answers and copied them. Key takeaway: guardrails removed the harm but did not, in this design, produce a gain.

**Stanford Tutor CoPilot (Wang et al. 2024).** 900 tutors, 1,800+ students; an LLM assistant coaching *human* tutors raised topic mastery by 4 points (62% to 66%), 9 points for lower-rated tutors, at $20/tutor/yr; the assistant nudged tutors toward guiding questions rather than answers ([Stanford SCALE](https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise), [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/what-happens-when-an-ai-assistant-helps-the-tutor-instead-of-the-student/2024/10)).

**Google LearnLM RCTs.** (a) UK, Nov 2025: 165 students, five schools, on the Eedi platform; LearnLM drafts supervised by expert tutors improved inter-topic transfer by +5.5 points vs. human-only, with 0 harmful messages and 5 factual errors in 3,617 drafts (0.1%) ([arXiv 2512.23633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633)). (b) Sierra Leone, published mid-2026: pre-registered RCT, 1,763 grade 7-8 students, 12 schools, 8 weeks; +0.258 SD on math (ITT); students with ≥12 hours of use moved roughly from the 50th to 64th percentile ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/measuring-the-impact-of-learning-with-ai-in-sierra-leone-and-beyond/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2026/07/02/google-tested-its-ai-tutor-in-real-classrooms-it-worked/)). Note: dosage-conditional figures are not causal.

**Meta-analyses.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 34 experiments reports large effects (0.795 cognitive; 0.711 competency), strongest "when LLMs function as tutors in sustained interventions" ([arXiv 2509.22725](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22725v1)); a 68-study review reports SMD 0.45. Publication bias and short durations are likely; treat these as upper bounds.

**Socratic-LLM research.** MathDial (2023) built a tutoring dialogue dataset from real teachers ([MathTutorBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18940v2)). MathTutorBench (EMNLP 2025) found that problem-solving skill "does not immediately translate to good teaching," that models strong at math are worse at "scaffolding a hint without revealing the answer," and that "tutoring becomes more challenging in longer dialogs, where simpler questioning strategies begin to fail" ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.18940)). SocraticLM (2024) uses a Dean-Teacher-Student multi-agent scheme with an LLM judge for Socratic quality ([SocraticMath](https://github.com/ECNU-ICALK/SocraticMath)). GuideEval / "Discerning minds or generic tutors?" (2025) found that "existing LLMs often fail to provide effective adaptive scaffolding when learners experience confusion," with the largest deficits in adapting elicitation after correct vs. incorrect answers ([arXiv 2508.06583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06583)). PEARL (May 2026, USTC/iFLYTEK) trains Socratic tutors with RL against a simulated student with latent misconceptions and a joint pedagogy+correctness reward; a 30B model matches proprietary LLMs ([arXiv 2605.29582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29582)). Together: generic "ask questions" prompting degrades in long dialogues and with confused learners; you need explicit learner-state inference and strategy switching.

**Over-reliance and cognitive offloading.** MIT "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (2025): 54 participants, EEG; LLM users showed the weakest neural connectivity, lowest essay ownership, and could not quote their own work; crucially, those who wrote unaided first and then used the LLM showed stronger recall than the reverse ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/), [Time](https://time.com/7295195/ai-chatgpt-google-learning-school/)). Microsoft/CMU CHI 2025 survey of 319 knowledge workers: higher confidence in the AI predicted less critical thinking; higher self-confidence predicted more ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713778)). "Faster Completion, Less Learning" (June 2026, working paper): 3.2M ALEKS interactions; study time on AI-susceptible problems fell 27-31% post-ChatGPT for college/high-school students, vanishing under proctoring ([arXiv 2605.21629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629), [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-eroding-math-skills/)). "The Effortless Trap" (June 2026) synthesises these into a placement rule: protect the Probe and Test phases from AI; treat effortlessness as a warning sign ([arXiv 2606.26181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26181)).

**What made the difference.** Across studies the positive results share: instructor-vetted content, answer-withholding with graded hints, brevity, frequent checks for understanding, and either human supervision or a pedagogically fine-tuned model. Negative results share unrestricted answer access and outcome measurement only during assisted practice.

## 3. Learning science that should shape the design

**Guidance for novices; fading with expertise.** Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006) argue minimal guidance fails for novices because working memory is overloaded by means-ends search; "the advantage of guidance begins to recede only when learners have sufficiently high prior knowledge" ([Kirschner et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1)). The worked-example effect and Kalyuga's expertise-reversal effect show worked examples beat problem solving for novices and reverse for advanced learners ([Kalyuga](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expertise_reversal_effect)). Implication: a tutor that only asks questions is doing minimal guidance. For a learner with no schema, the tutor should *teach briefly* (a compact explanation or worked example from the course text), then question. As the learner demonstrates knowledge, fade to problem-first.

**Productive failure, with a caveat for young children.** Sinha & Kapur's 2021 meta-analysis (53 studies) found problem-solving-before-instruction beats instruction-first with g = 0.36, rising to 0.58 with high design fidelity, but effects reversed for grades 2-5 and for domain-general skills, and scaffolding during the struggle phase did not help (g = −0.08) ([Sinha & Kapur](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105)). Implication: exploratory questioning before instruction is defensible for middle/high schoolers on concept-rich topics, but younger homeschoolers should get instruction first.

**Self-explanation.** Prompting learners to explain (why a step works, how it connects) yields g = 0.55 across 64 reports ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)). This is the Socratic tutor's strongest lever, and it works *after* a worked example as well as before.

**Retrieval, spacing, interleaving (Bjork's desirable difficulties).** Retrieval practice benefits are robust in classrooms (49 effect sizes, 57% medium-large) ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://remix.berklee.edu/faculty-works/12/)); spaced retrieval yields g = 0.74 vs. massed ([EPR 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1310148)); interleaving improves delayed transfer in math ([EPR 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10035-1)). Implication: the tutor's assessment should not happen only at the end of a lesson; each session should open with spaced retrieval of prior material and interleave older skills.

**Mastery learning.** d ≈ 0.52, strongest for weaker students ([Kulik et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265)). Implication: don't advance on a single correct answer; require demonstrated mastery across varied items.

**Formative assessment.** Black & Wiliam's 0.4-0.7 range was a narrative estimate; the first formal meta-analysis found ~0.20 (0.32 in ELA) ([Kingston & Nash 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2011.00220.x)). The mechanism that matters is feedback that changes the next instructional move.

**ICAP (Chi).** Interactive > constructive > active > passive engagement; the hierarchy's empirical basis has been questioned ([npj Science of Learning 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-023-00197-4)), but the constructive/interactive end (learner generating explanations, dialogue building on them) is consistent with the self-explanation evidence.

### Turn-by-turn implications for a Socratic tutor

1. **Diagnose before questioning.** The first two turns on a new concept should establish prior knowledge (a quick retrieval probe). If the learner is a novice, do not open with an open-ended question.
2. **Novice path: teach 30-60 seconds, then ask.** Deliver one worked example or a compact explanation from the course text, then ask the learner to explain a step or complete a faded step (completion problems).
3. **Intermediate path: question first, hint ladder second.** Ask; if wrong, respond with a reflective prompt, then a targeted hint, then a partial worked step. Never more than two consecutive questions without giving something back.
4. **Advanced path: problem-first, minimal hints, transfer questions.**
5. **Every 3-5 turns, a check for understanding** that requires production (explain, predict, apply) rather than recognition.
6. **Never end a session on struggle without consolidation:** a brief summary the learner produces, not the tutor.
7. **Spaced retrieval at session start**, interleaving one prior skill.
8. **Keep utterances short in voice.** Cognitive-load management was explicit in Kestin's prompt.

## 4. Recommendations

**What this product can do that others don't.**
- *Grounding in the parent's curriculum.* No mainstream guided tutor is bound to a family's own material with an auditable "did not assert outside the text" guarantee. Alpha School's failure mode (AI-generated lessons) and the 0.1% error rates Google achieved only with human supervision both argue for a retrieval-grounded tutor whose factual claims are traceable to the parent's chosen text.
- *A learner model that persists.* Generic modes have no prior-knowledge estimate, so they cannot implement expertise-reversal. The initial assessment and a per-concept mastery state let the orchestrator choose the novice/intermediate/advanced path per turn.
- *Voice-first Socratic dialogue for K-12 beyond math.* Synthesis proves demand for voice Socratic math; Khanmigo lacks real-time voice; big-lab voice modes are not curriculum-grounded. Voice also raises the cost of copy-pasting an answer, which is the behaviour Bastani's Base group exhibited.
- *Parent-facing mastery reports* from conversational assessment, replacing the auto-graded quizzes of Time4Learning-style platforms.

**Where Socratic is strongest.** Conceptual subjects and learners with some prior knowledge (middle/high school science, history reasoning, reading comprehension); self-explanation prompts after a worked example; metacognitive check-ins. Where it is weakest: complete novices, procedural fluency (arithmetic, phonics), and young children (grades K-5), where productive-failure effects reverse and worked examples dominate. The tutor should make the "teach then ask" hybrid its default, with pure questioning reserved for demonstrated-knowledge cases.

**Guardrails the evidence prioritises.**
1. Withhold final answers; graded hints from the course text (Bastani, Kestin).
2. Brevity, especially in voice (Kestin prompt).
3. Learner-state inference and strategy switching after correct vs. incorrect answers (GuideEval), with a multi-part-problem scaffold in the orchestrator, not the prompt (Kestin's limitation).
4. Protect the Probe and Test phases: the initial and mastery assessments must be AI-question, learner-answer only, with no hints (Effortless Trap; MIT ordering effect).
5. Time-on-task monitoring: a session that is faster than baseline is a red flag, not a win (Faster Completion, Less Learning).
6. Factual auditing against the source (the assessor role). Google's supervised setup hit 0.1% error; unsupervised models should be measured against that.
7. Parent controls on mode and transparency about what the tutor said (Alpha's covert monitoring backlash).

**What to measure in pilots.**
- *Unassisted delayed post-test* (1-2 weeks later), not in-session accuracy. The Bastani lesson.
- *Transfer* to novel problems on the same topic (LearnLM UK used +5.5 pp transfer).
- *Effect size vs. parent-led control* with a realistic target of 0.2-0.4 SD; pre-register.
- *Grounding error rate* per 1,000 tutor utterances, human-audited.
- *Answer-seeking rate*: fraction of learner turns asking for the answer, and tutor compliance rate (should be ~0).
- *Dialogue-quality metrics*: hint-ladder adherence, questions-per-turn, adaptivity after wrong answers (GuideEval-style rubric).
- *Retention and dosage*: hours/week and 8-week retention, since Sierra Leone gains were dosage-dependent.
- *Learner affect and parent trust*: engagement (Kestin's 4.1/5 benchmark), plus parent-reported time saved.

**Unverified items:** Synthesis exact pricing; whether Study Mode behaviour persists in ChatGPT voice; Gemini Guided Learning upload specifics; the "Kai Academy" product; OpenAI's 15% microeconomics result (self-reported); Alpha School tuition figures (secondary reporting).

# Part B. Voice architecture, latency, and curriculum ingestion

## 1. Hume EVI architecture and latency

**Speech-to-speech, not ASR+LLM+TTS (for the native path).** Hume describes EVI 3 as a "speech-language model" in which "a single autoregressive model processes both text and voice tokens," with a system prompt "comprised of both text and voice tokens" ([Hume: Introducing EVI 3](https://www.hume.ai/blog/introducing-evi-3), snippet). Hume claims EVI 3 "is capable of delivering voice responses in under 300ms on state-of-the-art hardware," while noting end-user latency depends on network and implementation (same post, snippet). Earlier generations: EVI 1 "<700 ms"; EVI 2 "500–800 ms" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/who-needs-gpt-4o-voice-mode-humes-evi-2-is-here-with-emotionally-inflected-voice-ai-and-api)). In Oct 2025 Hume also shipped **EVI 4 mini**, described as "40% faster with latency under 200 milliseconds" and roughly "100ms" faster per response than EVI 3; it pairs Octave 2 speech generation with a supplemental/external LLM rather than a native SLM ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/hume-ai-launches-octave-2-and-evi-4-mini-voice-models/), snippet; [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/sambanova-hume-ai-unleash-lightning-120000958.html)).

**What happens when you plug in an external model.** Hume's FAQ says that with a supplemental LLM "EVI transcribes user audio and extracts expression measures from the audio. The transcribed user message and expression measures are sent to the supplemental LLM, which generates a text response that is sent back to EVI," after which "EVI's speech-language model then voices the text, adjusting its tone, expressiveness, speaking rate ... think of EVI as a skilled actor 'acting out' the text" ([Hume FAQ](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/speech-to-speech-evi/faq), snippet). So on the external-LLM path EVI is effectively ASR + your LLM + Hume's expressive SLM-as-TTS. The EVI 3 API post adds that "when external LLMs are used, EVI 3 will generate the initial response to users and then allow the other LLM to take over and control the remainder of each response as soon as it can" ([Hume: EVI 3 API](https://www.hume.ai/blog/announcing-evi-3-api), snippet). Whether that "EVI speaks first, then hands off" behaviour also applies to CLM endpoints, or can be disabled, is **unverified**.

**CLM protocol (SSE flavour).** Per the CLM guide ([Hume docs](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/speech-to-speech-evi/guides/custom-language-model), snippet) and the official example ([hume-api-examples](https://github.com/HumeAI/hume-api-examples/blob/main/evi/evi-python-clm-sse/README.md)):

- EVI `POST`s to your URL, which "should start with https:// and end with /chat/completions".
- Body is OpenAI-chat-completions-shaped; the example reads `request_json["messages"]`. Hume's docs say implementations "extract only the role and content fields from each message" and that "Hume also supplies prosody information and other metadata," which you may "discard ... or attempt to reflect ... by modifying the messages."
- Shape of the prosody payload: the WebSocket example (`main.py` in [evi-python-clm-wss](https://github.com/HumeAI/hume-api-examples/tree/main/evi/evi-python-clm-wss)) reads `messages[].message.{role,content}` and `messages[].models.prosody.scores` (a label→float dict), sorts scores descending and appends the top two as text. The SSE example does not touch prosody. This repo's `apps/server/src/voice/clm.ts` assumes `models.prosody.scores` plus a possible inline `{confusion, interest}` suffix on the user text; the `time` field and the inline-suffix format are **unverified**.
- `custom_session_id` is passed "as a query parameter" on POST endpoints; Hume "recommend[s] passing a custom_session_id if you are using a Custom Language Model" ([session settings](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/speech-to-speech-evi/configuration/session-settings), snippet). For SSE responses "you can set the custom_session_id as a system_fingerprint on the ChatCompletion" and must not let OpenAI's own `system_fingerprint` leak through (CLM guide, snippet).
- Response: `text/event-stream` of `data: {chat.completion.chunk JSON}\n\n` ending with `data: [DONE]\n\n`.
- **Auth header:** the SDK's `SessionSettings.languageModelApiKey` is documented as "Third party API key for the supplemental language model. When provided, EVI will use this key instead of Hume's API key for the supplemental LLM" ([hume-typescript-sdk](https://github.com/HumeAI/hume-typescript-sdk/blob/main/src/api/resources/empathicVoice/types/SessionSettings.ts)). Whether the same key is forwarded to a CLM as an `Authorization: Bearer` header is **unverified**; the Python SSE example defines a bearer check but does not apply it to `/chat/completions`.
- **Streaming granularity:** Hume says "the key factor influencing perceived latency ... is the time to first token (TTFT)" and that SSE "ha[s] better latency properties" (Hume changelog and language-model page, snippets). That EVI begins synthesising on the first sentence rather than waiting for `[DONE]` is strongly implied by the streamed design but is not stated explicitly in anything reachable **(unverified)**. No published first-token target from Hume was found.
- **Interruptions:** EVI "detects the interruption, stops generating the response, and immediately notifies the client" with a `user_interruption` message ([interruptibility](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/speech-to-speech-evi/features/interruptibility), snippet). Whether Hume closes the in-flight CLM HTTP request on barge-in is **unverified**; `clm.ts` already aborts on socket close.
- **Note:** the "~200 ms first chunk" `instant_mode` figure that appears in Hume search results belongs to the TTS API, not EVI.

**CLM vs supplemental LLM.** Hume states that when it "connects to an upstream LLM provider directly ... this results in less latency compared to if Hume connects to your CLM which connects to an upstream LLM provider" (language-model page, snippet). CLM adds one network hop and your server's own overhead. The upside of CLM is full control: proprietary prompts, RAG, and orchestration "without the need to modify the system prompt."

**Claude as a native supplemental LLM.** Hume "made Claude the default supplemental LLM for EVI" ([Hume blog](https://www.hume.ai/blog/hume-anthropic-claude-voice-interactions), snippet); the case study reports prompt caching "reduce[d] costs by 80% and decrease[d] latency by more than 10%" ([Claude customers: Hume](https://claude.com/customers/hume), snippet). The May 2026 changelog adds `claude-opus-4-6` and "zero prompt expansion" so you control the full system prompt for external LLMs ([releasebot](https://releasebot.io/updates/hume), snippet). User-defined tools are supported in the config/session `tools` field. Whether the supplemental-LLM list in Sept 2026 includes Opus/Sonnet 5 is **unverified**.

## 2. Conversational latency benchmarks

**Human baseline.** Stivers et al. (PNAS 2009) found across 10 languages a "unimodal distribution with the highest number of transitions occurring between 0 and 200 ms," overall mode ~0 ms ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106)). Levinson's follow-up notes production latency is ">600 ms," implying speakers plan during the incoming turn ([Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.268)). Practically, a ~200–500 ms gap reads as natural; industry writing repeats "600 ms feels natural, 800 ms feels like it is thinking" ([enterprisedna](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/openai-gpt-realtime-2-1-voice-agents-enterprise-july-2026/), snippet).

**Vendor claims and measurements (end of speech → first audio):**

| System | Claimed | Independently measured |
|---|---|---|
| Hume EVI 3 | "<300 ms" (snippet) | none found |
| Hume EVI 4 mini | "<200 ms" (snippet) | none found |
| OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.1 (Jul 2026) | time-to-first-audio 0.97 s (minimal effort) → 1.21 s (high) ([handyai](https://handyai.substack.com/p/model-drop-gpt-realtime-2), snippet) | ~500 ms TTFB from US clients; "800 ms voice-to-voice when wired right" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api), snippet) |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash Live (native audio) | "sub-800ms" first audio | 320–800 ms in a tutorial ([lablab](https://lablab.ai/ai-tutorials/building-voice-agents-gemini-live-fastapi)); Sept 2026 forum reports of 9–15 s regressions ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-1-flash-live-preview-9-15-s-time-to-first-audio-since-2026-09-05-09-00-utc-was-1-s-some-turns-never-answer-reproduced-from-two-projects-two-networks/180918)) |
| ElevenLabs Conversational AI | TTS ~75 ms (Flash), excludes STT/LLM | median full-turn 1,424 ms, p95 1,768 ms ([openbenchmarks](https://openbenchmarks.com/voice-agent-latency/elevenlabs-vs-vapi), snippet) |
| Vapi | "sub-500 ms" | median 1,558 ms, p95 2,008 ms (same source) |

**Claude time-to-first-token (Artificial Analysis, Anthropic first-party endpoint, snippets from [artificialanalysis.ai](https://artificialanalysis.ai/providers/anthropic)):** Haiku 4.5 ~0.71–0.74 s; Sonnet 4.5 1.77 s; Opus 4.7 (non-reasoning) 1.00 s; Sonnet 5 "time to first answer token" 1.38 s at low effort (another snippet says 0.83 s; treat as ~0.8–1.4 s); Opus 5 low effort 2.67 s. A separate benchmark reports Haiku 4.5 at 597 ms on medium prompts ([aimultiple](https://aimultiple.com/llm-latency-benchmark), snippet). Implication: with `claude-opus-5` as the live tutor, first-token alone exceeds the entire human turn gap; Sonnet 5 at low effort or Haiku 4.5 is the realistic voice tier.

**Techniques.** (a) Prompt caching: one measurement shows TTFT reductions "from 4% at a 20,000-token system prompt to 52% at 160,000 tokens" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@AdithyaGiridharan/anthropic-is-quietly-building-an-inference-control-plane-prompt-cache-pre-warming-is-the-latest-f0b6a4b90919), snippet); keep the stable prefix byte-identical. (b) Effort/thinking: `output_config.effort` trades tokens for latency; for voice use low effort. Anthropic's own latency page recommends Haiku 4.5, short outputs, `max_tokens`, and streaming ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-latency)). (c) Sentence-level flushing: "stream tokens out of the LLM and feed them into TTS as they arrive, chunked at sentence or clause boundaries" ([Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/learn/voice-agent-architecture-stt-llm-tts-pipeline-design), snippet). (d) Filler/backchannel: "if the response is not ready within a fixed interval, emit a short acknowledgment ... a policy decision the orchestrator enforces" ([Hamming](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-interruption-handling-runbook), snippet). (e) Speculative response: "feed stable partials into the LLM and revise if the final transcript differs" ([Picovoice](https://picovoice.ai/guide/voice-agents/voice-ux-latency-turn-taking/), snippet; see also [RelayS2S](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23346)). (f) Barge-in: stop playback at the media layer and cancel in-flight generation.

## 3. Ingesting video, podcasts and slides

**YouTube.**
- *Official API:* `captions.download` costs 200 quota units and "requires an OAuth token from an account that owns the video" ([youtube2text](https://youtube2text.org/blog/youtube-data-api-transcripts), snippet; [Google](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/download)). Useless for third-party videos.
- *yt-dlp* (`--write-auto-subs --skip-download`) works but "using yt-dlp to download from YouTube violates the ToS — ... not a criminal matter"; remedy is account/civil action ([sky-scribe](https://www.sky-scribe.com/en/blog/yt-dlp-risks-legal-safety-transcription), snippet). Recommend only for a family's own or clearly licensed content, ideally run locally rather than from a server IP.
- *`youtube-transcript` npm / `youtubei.js` (InnerTube):* unofficial; scraping "sometimes fails," forks add InnerTube fallback ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@danielxceron/youtube-transcript), snippet); cloud IPs are frequently blocked ([youtube-transcript-api issue](https://github.com/jdepoix/youtube-transcript-api/issues/303)).
- *Hosted transcript APIs:* Supadata from "$5 for 300 credits" with an AI-STT fallback; SearchApi "$40/mo for ~5K searches" ([transcriptapi](https://transcriptapi.com/blog/youtube-transcript-api-comparison), snippet). They shift the ToS/blocking risk to the vendor but do not remove it.

**TED.** TED talks, including transcripts, are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; transcripts "may be used under the same Creative Commons license in conjunction with the TED Talk video," and structured curricular use "may require a separate license" ([TED usage policy](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-talks-usage-policy); [Newcastle guide](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/876017/Understanding-Copyright-Using-TED-Talks.pdf), snippets). Technically, ted.com pages embed talk data in a `__NEXT_DATA__` blob and scrapers return cues with `start_ms/duration_ms/text` ([Apify](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/ted-talks-transcript-scraper), snippet). yt-dlp has a TED extractor, but it broke on 21 Aug 2026 (PR #17510 pending) ([yt-dlp issue](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/17507)).

**Podcasts.** Parse the RSS `<enclosure>`; check first for a `<podcast:transcript>` tag (Podcasting 2.0 namespace, `text/vtt`, `application/srt`, `application/json`) before paying for STT ([podcast-namespace](https://github.com/Podcastindex-org/podcast-namespace/blob/main/docs/1.0.md)). Enclosures are publicly distributed by the publisher, so fetching for personal educational use is far lower risk than YouTube scraping.

**Speech-to-text (Sept 2026, all snippets):** Deepgram Nova-3 $0.0043/min batch (~$0.26/hr), ~$0.29/hr streaming, $200 free credit; independent WER 5.26% batch / 6.84% streaming ([convertaudiototext](https://convertaudiototext.com/blog/deepgram-nova-3-explained); [brasstranscripts](https://brasstranscripts.com/blog/deepgram-pricing-per-minute-2025-real-time-vs-batch); [futureagi](https://futureagi.substack.com/p/speech-to-text-apis-in-2026-benchmarks)). AssemblyAI Universal-3 $0.15/hr async, Universal-3.5 Pro $0.21/hr async, $0.45/hr realtime ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-transcription-apis/assemblyai/); [AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/how-accurate-speech-to-text)). OpenAI `gpt-4o-transcribe` ~$0.006/min (token-priced), `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` ~$0.003/min, `whisper-1` $0.006/min ([costgoat](https://costgoat.com/pricing/openai-transcription)). Google Chirp 3 $0.016/min standard ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-transcription-apis/google-speech-to-text/)). Local: faster-whisper "up to 4x" reference speed; large-v3-turbo ~5–8x faster than large-v3 at near-identical English accuracy ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/faster-whisper/); [codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/faster-whisper-vs-whisper-cpp-speech-to-text-2026/)).

**PowerPoint.** Node: `officeParser` (jszip-based) parses PPTX into an AST with slide text and speaker notes ([GitHub](https://github.com/harshankur/officeParser)); lighter alternatives are hand-rolled jszip over `ppt/slides/slideN.xml` and `ppt/notesSlides/` (what this repo does). Python: `python-pptx`.

## Recommended default stack (research conclusion)

1. **Voice:** keep Hume EVI 3 (or EVI 4 mini for lower latency) in **CLM mode** hitting the existing `/clm/chat/completions` SSE bridge; it is the only way to keep the orchestrator/grounding/assessor loop authoritative. Accept the extra hop; compensate with Sonnet 5 low-effort or Haiku 4.5 for the tutor turn, prompt caching on the stable prefix, streaming every delta, an orchestrator-enforced filler after ~700 ms if measurements demand it, and abort-on-close.
2. **YouTube:** prefer a hosted transcript API for public videos with an explicit "own/licensed content only" policy; fall back to local yt-dlp. Avoid server-side yt-dlp against YouTube in production.
3. **TED:** read the transcript from the talk page, cache with attribution, and gate on NC/ND terms; expect breakage.
4. **Podcasts:** RSS → `<podcast:transcript>` if present → else Deepgram Nova-3 batch or local faster-whisper large-v3-turbo.
5. **PPTX:** jszip over slide XML, feeding the heading-aware chunker with slide numbers as headings.
